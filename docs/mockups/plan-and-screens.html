Battery against VLC: the estimate

Worked out, not measured: from what each app does every second during a film, the Snapdragon 8 Gen 2's published figures and AMOLED panel behaviour. An emulator can't help here (no hardware decoder, no power model). Treat the numbers as ±30–50 %; Gate 2 on the Thor gives the real ones. 1 % of the 6,000 mAh battery per hour ≈ 0.23 W.

Test day on the Thor: battery and heat

  • Same 1-hour 1080p HEVC file, charged to 100 %, brightness 50 %, Wi-Fi off, Performance mode Standard, same firmware.
  • Make the bottom screen fair: VLC with AYN's bottom-screen-off, Exo Player with ⏻. Write down how each ran.
  • Note the battery drop for each, and at 30 and 60 minutes: the fan (did it spin, when), how warm the back and grips are, the temperature if the Thor shows one, and any stutter.
  • If one runs hotter, check the diagnostics page (Select + Start) for a file decoded in software: the usual cause, 0.5–1.5 W more.

Battery ideas, held until the test

Small, because the expensive parts are already done the cheap way. Built only if the test shows they're needed.

  • Bottom screen goes fully black during playback and stops redrawing: ≈ 0.1–0.2 % per hour.
  • Top screen at 48 Hz for 24 fps films, if the Thor offers it: ≈ 0.02–0.05 W, and no judder.
  • A "Software decoding" warning in the player: avoids the biggest single drain.
  • ExoPlayer's dynamic scheduling (experimental): a few mW.
  • "Play HDR as SDR": large, on HDR films only; later.
  • Do Not Disturb while a film plays; a list of apps that keep running, to set to Restricted. (Killing other apps doesn't help: Android only lets an app close idle ones, and they restart.)

Bigger savings are in the Thor's settings: brightness 50 → 30 % (≈ 1 % per hour), headphones instead of speakers (≈ 0.5–1 %), fan off in Standard mode, airplane mode.
